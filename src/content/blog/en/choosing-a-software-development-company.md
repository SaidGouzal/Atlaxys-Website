Choosing a software partner is one of the most expensive decisions a growing company makes — and one of the hardest to evaluate, because the output is invisible until it is too late. Portfolios and pitch decks tell you little. **How a team works** tells you almost everything.

These twelve questions work whether you are comparing agencies in Casablanca, freelancers in Lisbon or consultancies in Montreal.

## Understanding

**1. Did they ask more questions than they answered?** A good team spends the first meeting trying to understand your business, users and constraints. A team that proposes a solution in the first thirty minutes is selling, not engineering.

**2. Can they explain your problem back to you?** Ask them to summarise the project in writing. If the summary is vague or generic, the estimate will be too.

**3. Do they tell you what not to build?** Pushing back on scope is a sign of experience. Agreeing to everything is a sign of an estimate that will grow.

## Engineering

**4. Who will actually do the work?** Meet the engineers, not only the sales lead. Ask about their seniority and how many other projects they are on.

**5. How do they make and record technical decisions?** Look for written architecture documents or decision records. "We'll use the latest framework" is not an architecture.

**6. How do they test and release?** Ask about code review, automated tests, staging environments and how a release reaches production. Manual, late-night deployments are a warning sign.

**7. How do they handle security and personal data?** They should talk comfortably about access control, secrets management, backups, and regulations such as Morocco's Law 09-08 or the GDPR.

## Delivery

**8. How often will you see working software?** The right answer is every one or two weeks, on an environment you can use yourself — not a slide with a percentage.

**9. How are changes handled?** Scope will change. There should be a simple, written process for estimating and approving changes.

**10. What happens after launch?** Maintenance, support, monitoring and response times should be defined before you sign, not negotiated during an outage.

## Ownership

**11. Who owns the code, the accounts and the data?** The answer should be "you, from day one": repositories in your organisation, cloud accounts in your name, domains registered to you.

**12. Could another team take over tomorrow?** Ask to see the documentation of a past project. If a new engineer could not run the system from the README, you would be locked in.

## A simple scoring method

Score each question from 0 to 2 for every candidate, and weight "Ownership" and "Delivery" double. The totals will not make the decision for you — but they turn a gut feeling into a conversation your team can have with evidence.

## Before you sign

- Start with a **paid discovery phase** or a small first milestone. It is the cheapest way to test the relationship.
- Make sure the contract covers **intellectual property, acceptance criteria and exit terms**.
- Keep a **single point of contact** on your side who can make decisions quickly.
