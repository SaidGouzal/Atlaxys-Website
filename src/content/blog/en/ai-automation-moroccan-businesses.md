Artificial intelligence is on every conference agenda in Casablanca and Rabat, but most Moroccan SMEs ask a simpler question: **what can it actually do for my business this quarter?** The answer is usually less spectacular than a chatbot demo — and far more useful.

## Start with the process, not the model

The biggest gains rarely come from AI alone. They come from removing manual steps between tools that already exist: the email that becomes a spreadsheet row, the spreadsheet that becomes an invoice, the invoice that becomes an accounting entry. Automating those handovers often needs no AI at all — just reliable integrations.

AI earns its place where the input is **unstructured**: scanned documents, free-text emails, voice notes, photos. That is where rules alone cannot cope.

## Five good first candidates

1. **Supplier invoices and receipts.** Extract amounts, VAT, dates and supplier details, validate them, and prepare the accounting entry.
2. **Customer requests arriving by WhatsApp and email.** Classify, extract the order or complaint details, and route to the right person with context.
3. **Weekly and monthly reporting.** Pull figures from sales, stock and finance tools into one report — automatically, every time.
4. **Order and delivery reconciliation.** Match orders, deliveries and payments across platforms and flag only the exceptions.
5. **Internal knowledge.** An assistant that answers staff questions from your own procedures, price lists and contracts — with links to the source.

## The Moroccan specifics

### Languages

Moroccan businesses work across French, Modern Standard Arabic, Darija and often English — sometimes in the same document. Modern language models handle French and Arabic well; Darija, especially written in Latin script, is harder. **Test on your real documents and messages in every language you receive**, and keep a human review step for the cases where confidence is low.

### WhatsApp is a business channel

For many Moroccan companies, WhatsApp is where orders, questions and confirmations actually happen. Automation projects that ignore it miss the main source of work. The WhatsApp Business Platform allows compliant, structured integrations with your systems.

### Data protection under Law 09-08

Personal data in Morocco is protected by **Law 09-08**, overseen by the **CNDP**, which also regulates transfers of personal data outside the country. Before sending documents to any AI service:

- send only the data the task genuinely needs;
- choose providers and settings that do not use your data for training;
- document the processing, and declare it where required;
- consider keeping the most sensitive processing on infrastructure you control.

This is not legal advice — involve your legal counsel — but designing for privacy from the start is far cheaper than retrofitting it.

## Measure before you trust

An AI step should be treated like any other component that can fail:

- Build a **labelled sample** of real documents or messages — a few hundred is often enough to start.
- Measure **accuracy per field and per language** before rollout.
- Set a **confidence threshold**; below it, a person reviews.
- **Log every automated decision** so errors can be traced and corrected.
- Track **cost per document** so the automation stays profitable as volume grows.

## A realistic roadmap

1. **Weeks 1–2:** map the process as it actually runs, pick one high-volume task, collect a sample.
2. **Weeks 3–6:** build the pipeline and review interface, measure accuracy on the sample.
3. **Weeks 7–10:** pilot with one team, alongside the existing process.
4. **After:** relax review thresholds where accuracy holds, then move to the next process.

## The takeaway

AI automation in Morocco works best when it is boring: one process at a time, measured, reversible and designed for the languages and channels your business actually uses. Start small, prove the value on your own data, then scale.
