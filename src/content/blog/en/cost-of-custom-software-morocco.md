"How much will it cost?" is the first question almost every client asks — and the honest answer is always "it depends". This article explains **what it depends on**, so you can read an estimate critically and compare proposals on something other than the bottom line.

## The formula behind every estimate

Whatever the pricing model on the contract, the cost of custom software comes down to one calculation:

> **people × time × rate** — plus the costs that do not stop at launch.

- **People** — how many engineers, designers and project leads, and how senior.
- **Time** — how many weeks of work the scope requires, including discovery, testing and deployment.
- **Rate** — the daily or monthly cost of each person, which varies with seniority and with the market the team works in.

Rates in Morocco are generally lower than in Western Europe or North America for comparable seniority, which is one reason companies from those markets work with Moroccan teams. But rate is the variable you control least and that matters least. **Scope and execution move the total far more.**

## What drives the scope

Two projects that both call themselves "a management app" can differ by a factor of ten. The drivers:

1. **Number of user roles and workflows.** Each role (admin, manager, field staff, customer) multiplies screens, permissions and edge cases.
2. **Integrations.** Payment providers, accounting software, government services, ERPs and legacy databases are often the riskiest part of a project, because they depend on systems you do not control.
3. **Platforms.** A web application, a mobile app, a desktop application or all three.
4. **Data migration.** Moving years of data out of spreadsheets or an old system is its own project.
5. **Offline use, real-time features and scale.** Each adds architecture work and testing.
6. **Languages and right-to-left support.** An interface in French and Arabic needs more than translated strings; layouts, dates, numbers and search all need attention.
7. **Compliance and security.** Handling personal data under Morocco's Law 09-08, or GDPR for European users, adds process and design work.

## Typical project shapes

Rather than invented average prices, here are the shapes we see most often. Put your own rates against the durations to get a first order of magnitude.

| Shape | Typical scope | Typical team | Typical duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Focused tool | One or two workflows, one role, a handful of integrations | 1–2 engineers | 4–8 weeks |
| Business platform | Several roles, reporting, 2–4 integrations, web + mobile or desktop | 2–4 people | 3–6 months |
| Product / SaaS | Multi-tenant, billing, public users, scale and security requirements | 4+ people | 6 months and ongoing |

Durations include discovery, testing and deployment — not just coding.

## How to read an estimate

A trustworthy estimate is a document, not a number. Look for:

- **A feature breakdown with ranges.** Single numbers suggest false precision.
- **Explicit assumptions.** "The accounting software offers an API" is an assumption that can double a budget if it turns out to be wrong.
- **A list of unknowns and how they will be resolved** — often through a short, paid discovery phase.
- **What is excluded.** Content entry, data migration, app-store fees, hosting and third-party licences are commonly left out.
- **The cost of running the system afterwards.**

If two proposals differ widely, the gap is almost always in the assumptions. Ask each vendor to list theirs.

## Five ways to keep the budget under control

1. **Pay for discovery first.** A few weeks of workshops and architecture work, delivered as a written scope, is the cheapest insurance you can buy.
2. **Ship a thin slice early.** One complete workflow in production teaches more than months of specification.
3. **Phase the project.** Each phase has its own budget and its own useful outcome, so you can stop, adjust or continue.
4. **Say no to "while we're at it".** Keep a backlog for later phases instead of growing the current one.
5. **Own your code and accounts from day one.** It keeps your options open and prevents expensive lock-in.

## The bottom line

The price of custom software in Morocco — or anywhere — is set by scope and by the quality of execution far more than by the hourly rate. Treat estimates as a set of assumptions to challenge, invest in discovery, and plan for life after launch.
